Listening for Guidance

How one Quaker learned to hear the Spirit, and how you can begin

From the writing of Jeff Kisling
Before you beginListening for Guidance

A thing we have no words for

Jeff Kisling is a Quaker. He is 75. He has tried for most of his life to listen for the Spirit and to do what he hears.

This book tells some of his stories. It does not give you rules. It gives you words for things that may already happen to you, and some ways to practice.

How to read it

Read one page. Stop. Sit with it. Quakers learn slowly, by doing, beside people who have done it for a long time.

1. The groundListening for Guidance

That of God in everyone

Quakers believe there is that of God in every person. That idea changes everything about listening.

You are spoken to

If the Spirit is in everyone, the Spirit speaks to everyone. You do not have to be old, calm or holy.

The hard part is hearing

A leading rarely feels like a big moment. It often feels like a small interest that will not go away.

The harder part is following

Many people notice. Fewer people act. The first step is usually small.

1. The groundListening for Guidance

Where he learned to listen

Jeff grew up in Bear Creek Meeting, a rural Quaker community in Iowa. He heard stories of Friends who went to prison rather than go to war.

In 1970 he graduated from Scattergood Friends School, a Quaker boarding school on a farm.

Nobody taught it as a subject. Students learned it by living it, working on the farm, and sitting in silence together.

2. The practiceListening for Guidance

The silent hour

In unprogrammed Quaker worship there is no minister and no sermon. People sit together in silence for about an hour.

The silence is not empty. It is practice. You practice sitting still, waiting, and receiving something you did not make yourself.

Some Sundays several people speak. Some Sundays no one does. Both are good.

2. The practiceListening for Guidance

Expectant waiting

When you sit down, your mind is busy. That is normal. Let the noise go quiet slowly. Friends call this centering down.

Some Friends say there is a deeper place below centering down. There you are not only calm. You are listening.

Two words, two jobs

Waiting means you do not force an answer. Expectant means you really believe something may come, so you pay attention.

2. The practiceListening for Guidance

Listening all week

Every morning Jeff sits quietly to discern what to write that day. He does not just decide.

He has no car, so he walks. He carries a camera and looks closely. He has kept journals since he was 17.

2. The practiceListening for Guidance

Prayer that moves

In December 2023, Jeff's mother died. A friend told him she was sending prayers.

The next morning he woke at 5 am. The moonlight was very bright, and he could not sleep. He remembered another dawn, when he was led to walk to Picnic Point.

So he went out into the cold, in the dark, and saw an extraordinary sunrise.

There was no voice. There was light, a memory, and a small pull to go outside.

3. When guidance comesListening for Guidance

What is a leading?

Each part of that sentence rules something out.

1
It arrives. You did not reason your way to it.
So it is not just a good idea.
2
You had no way of knowing you wanted it.
So it is not a preference.
3
It asks for something you would not choose.
So it is not just convenient.
4
It stays until you obey or refuse it.
So it is not a passing impulse.

A leading is not a plan. It shows a direction and a small next step, not the end.

3. When guidance comesListening for Guidance

The second email

Around 2012 Jeff found a youth center in Indianapolis, the Kheprw Institute. Young people there were building rain barrels and fish-and-plant gardens.

He sent an email. Nobody answered. He almost gave up. But he wrote that

So he sent a second email. On a dark, rainy night he rode his bicycle to meet them.

Try to remember this

When something will not leave you alone, and the first door stays shut, knock again.

3. When guidance comesListening for Guidance

“And you, and you”

At the Kheprw Institute, a group of teens and leaders asked Jeff to tell them about himself. Then they asked for more, and more. It was an interview.

He did not know what to say next. Then, he says, the Inner Light told him.

Each person smiled. He wrote: “I strongly felt the presence of the Spirit among us.” Years later, one of the leaders said of him: “he listened.”

4. Testing what you hearListening for Guidance

A choice at eighteen

During the Vietnam War, every young man had to register for the draft. Jeff was a student at Scattergood. He was afraid. He was also afraid of a wrong motive: wanting people to admire him.

He registered on November 26, 1969. On January 2, 1970, he returned his certificate to the draft board. His parents were very upset. On February 6, 1972, he broke with Selective Service by letter.

4. Testing what you hearListening for Guidance

Test what you hear

Quakers do not believe a feeling proves itself. They test it, often with other people.

The clearness committee

A few people sit with you. They give no advice. They only ask honest, open questions, and they wait while you answer.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Would I still do this if nobody ever found out?
  • Does it cost me something real?
  • Would I have chosen it if someone just asked what I wanted?
  • Does it ask other people to change, or me?

5. Listening through other peopleListening for Guidance

Listening through other people

In September 2018, Native and non-Native people walked 94 miles together along the Dakota Access Pipeline in Iowa. They walked to share stories. Jeff walked too.

His Native friends taught him that the land is our relative. At one point where they crossed over the pipeline, Jeff was asked to offer prayers. He explained Quaker worship, and the group held hands in the silence for a time.

This guidance did not come in a silent room. It came through walking, through friends, and through hearing stories he could not learn from a book.

3. When guidance comesListening for Guidance

When it is hard

Sometimes the silence feels empty. Keep coming anyway. A seed under the ground does not look busy, but it grows.

In late 2025, Jeff felt overwhelmed. He could not sleep. He felt a strong leading and also felt alone in it. He stayed away from meeting for several months.

A care committee is a few Friends who walk beside you. If listening becomes too much, step back and let other people help carry it.

15
6. For youListening for Guidance

Try this

You do not have to be a Quaker to try these steps. Do one step for a month.

  1. 1Put your phone in another room for 20 minutes. Sit in silence.
  2. 2Hold one question: What is being asked of me? Do not answer it. Notice what comes.
  3. 3Write down the thing that keeps coming back. Read your notes again after two weeks.
  4. 4Walk without headphones. Look closely at one place each day.
  5. 5When something will not leave you alone, take one small step in the next 48 hours.
  6. 6Ask an older Friend: “When did you feel led? What did it cost?”
  7. 7When a community asks for help, go. Do the work they ask for. Listen before you speak.
